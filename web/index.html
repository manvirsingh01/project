Model notes

Method & sources

The assumptions and supplied benchmark anchors behind this simulator.

A model for the edge, not a crypto library.

This simulator reproduces and interpolates benchmark information supplied in the repository design notes. It does not generate keys, signatures, ciphertexts, or run real cryptographic code.

⌁

Why the resource budget matters

IoT devices have fixed CPU, RAM, flash, energy, and radio capacity. Post-quantum algorithms can change the balance between compute and message size, so this tool helps compare projected trade-offs before choosing target hardware.

Benchmarking approach

  1. Cycle-accurate profiling: stored time anchors and clock scaling stand in for cycle counts.
  2. Zero-wait-state execution: reported setups are retained; cache and flash wait states are not modeled.
  3. Statistical variance: CV and published p99 are stored; p50/p95 use a disclosed log-normal approximation with a percentile fallback.
  4. Static flash tracking: one firmware footprint field is defined; numeric source values are unavailable.
  5. Dynamic stack instrumentation: supplied peak-stack anchors feed the MWC assessment.
  6. Empirical energy: energy anchors scale with projected time.
  7. Cross-architecture KAT equivalence: unsupported; this tool produces no cryptographic outputs.
  8. Network fragmentation: 6LoWPAN frames and independent-loss success are modeled.
  9. Side-channel leakage analysis: unsupported; qualitative mention only.
  10. Hardware/software area-time profiling: unsupported; FPGA/ASIC area is out of scope.

What the anchors say

  • ML-KEM-512 on Cortex-M0+ at 133 MHz: 35.7 ms, 9.4 KB peak stack, and 2.83 mJ.
  • ML-KEM-768: 56.6 ms and 14.2 KB stack; ML-KEM-1024: 85.7 ms.
  • ML-DSA-44 signing: 158.9 ms mean, 67.5% coefficient of variation, 489.9 ms p99, and a 2,420-byte signature.
  • ML-DSA-87 signing: 355.2 ms mean, 119.6 KB peak stack, 1,125 ms p99, and a 4,627-byte signature.
  • Available estimates are sparse. Missing measurements are deliberately shown as unavailable.

Projection math & confidence

Reference time anchors are treated as M0+ at 133 MHz. Time scales linearly by clock ratio and by a 1.85× M4/M7 speedup assumption. Stack is copied from its anchor. Energy scales with time only when a time anchor is available. Variance uses σ = ln(1 + CV/100), derives p50/p95, and checks modeled p99 against the reported p99. A mismatch above 15% switches to percentile interpolation.

Direct RP2040 @133 MHz values are marked measured, same-family clock projections interpolated, cross-family projections extrapolated, and algorithms without numeric timing unsupported. Device clocks absent from the notes use a nominal 133 MHz timing assumption.

Minimum Workable Configuration

  • 32-bit Cortex-M0+ or better at 48 MHz or faster.
  • 64 KB RAM for KEM-only use; 128 KB for local signing workflows.
  • 512 KB minimum flash; 1 MB is the recommended OTA target.
  • Below 80% of the RAM threshold is infeasible. Below the threshold or recommended flash is marginal.

The dedicated 16 KB crypto-RAM target mentioned in the notes is not separately modeled.

Network model

IEEE 802.15.4/6LoWPAN uses 81 effective payload bytes per single-hop frame and 45 bytes for multi-hop. The design notes give an MTU of 127 bytes. Fragment count is the ceiling of payload size divided by effective frame payload. One lost fragment drops the whole datagram, and independent-loss probability is used for the success estimate.

LoRa payload/frame capacity is a user-editable assumption, defaulting to 51 bytes, since no LoRa constant was included in the supplied notes.

Cache, alerts & provenance

Shared calculations use an in-process 500-entry LRU cache. CLI results can persist in the local versioned cache and run history. Alerts flag MWC failures, tail latency, low network reliability, fragmentation, confidence drops, and recorded regressions. Nothing is sent externally unless a CLI webhook URL is explicitly supplied.

Energy/time scaling is approximate; RF loss may be correlated; side-channel and hardware-accelerator benchmarking remain unsupported. The reference index and benchmark crosswalk are included below.
